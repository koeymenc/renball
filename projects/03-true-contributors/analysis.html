RESEARCH · 03 May 2026 Top 5 Leagues + Eredivisie Player Value EP Model

The True Contributors.

The standard scoreboard is generous to the prolific and unkind to the subtle. Goals and assists get name-checked; pre-assists, late equalisers and high-leverage build-up play disappear into the squad page. This project applies the expected-points value model from project 02 to seven seasons of Europe's six biggest leagues — and credits three touchpoints per goal (scorer, assist-creator, pre-assist-creator) — to find the players the box score systematically undervalues.

1 · The framework

For every goal in the dataset, project 02 computes expected points (EP) collected — the swing in win/draw/loss probability before and after the goal, conditioned on the scoreline and the minute bin. A late equaliser is worth almost a full league point; a 4–0 consolation, almost nothing. This project extends that credit to three roles per goal — the scorer, the assist-creator (SCA1) and the pre-assist-creator (SCA2) — then asks who the model elevates that the box score does not.

2 · Per-90, full credit · the model agrees with the eye test

Open with the leaderboard. Each goal credits up to three players equally (scorer + SCA1 + SCA2 = 1 each). Sum the EP, normalise per 90 minutes, filter to ≥ 1,000 minutes played and Top-5 leagues only. The top of the list is Messi, Mbappé, Neymar — exactly who you'd expect, which is the point. Before we show you the surprises, the framework has to clear the eye test.

Does weighting the credits change the answer?

Maybe a finishing touch is worth more than a pre-assist. Re-run the same leaderboard with credits weighted 0.40 / 0.35 / 0.25 (scorer / SCA1 / SCA2) — only the EP is weighted, the qualifying-minutes threshold doesn't change. The result: the top eight is essentially identical. The biggest movers under weighting are pure scorers gaining a few places (Lewandowski +6, Dovbyk +7) and a few creator-heavy names losing a few (Jallow −9, Ljajić −9). Conclusion: the simpler 1/1/1 model is doing the same work as the carefully-tuned weights, which is reassuring.

Under credit weighting Robert Lewandowski jumps 6 ranks and Artem Dovbyk jumps 7 — the model rewards consistent scorers more than fleeting playmakers when you give scoring extra weight. The fact that this only moves the list by a handful of positions is itself the finding.

4 · The late-game substantives

Of every player's total EP from goals (across all three roles), what share is collected in the last 30 minutes? Restrict to players with ≥ 30 credits across the dataset and the super-sub cohort drops away. Cristian Tello at Betis tops the list — 73.8% of his contribution comes after the 60th minute across 44 credits. Yoane Wissa, Ángel Rodríguez, Adama Traoré, Giacomo Raspadori all in the same bracket: high-volume, high-leverage closers.

Top 10 by late share, ≥ 30 three-layer credits. Late = EP collected in 60-75 + 75-90 bins. Includes Eredivisie (no per-90 dependency). Players with high late share but low credit volume (super-sub specialists) are excluded from this view — see the full data in the dashboard.

5 · Efficiency ratios

Two ways a player can be "efficient" in this framework: their EP per credit (do they score high-leverage goals?) and their time-aware multiplier (how much does the model upgrade them vs the time-blind baseline?). Both filtered to ≥ 20 credits to keep the noise out.

EP per credit (≥ 20 credits). Higher = scores/creates in higher-leverage moments per goal.
Time-aware multiplier = ep_timed / ep_untimed (≥ 20 credits). Higher = the time dimension upgrades them more.

Minutes shown so readers can self-assess sample size. Players at < 2,000 mins (half-season) are flagged in muted text. Top 5 leagues only — ≥ 1,000 minutes played.

The model has no opinion on goalkeepers. The EP framework credits scorers, assist-creators and pre-assist-creators — three touchpoints GKs occupy rarely. In this dataset the top GK posts ~0.13 EP/90 against ~1.14 for the top forward, an order-of-magnitude gap that reflects the framework's coverage, not the keepers' actual value. Quantifying GK contribution requires a different model (shot-stopping over expected, distribution value); we defer it to a future project.

7 · The Hidden Architects

Here is the discovery. SCA2 — the pre-assist-creator, the player whose pass set up the pass that set up the goal — is the layer of contribution football's mainstream stats rarely credit. The model does. Stack the top 10 SCA2 EP collectors and segment each bar by where their EP came from (scorer / SCA1 / SCA2 in three colours), and one player is structurally different from everyone else.
