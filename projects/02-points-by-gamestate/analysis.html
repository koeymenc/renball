Not All Goals Are Equal.

A goal from 0–0 to 1–0 in the fifth minute and the same goal in the eighty-fifth are not the same thing. One opens a match; the other nearly closes it. Expected points (EP) prices every goal by what it moved: the points a team could expect at that score in that part of the match, before and after the ball went in. The prices come from every minute of every match, so a 0–0 that stays 0–0 counts too.

Level late: upside vs downside

The price of a goal depends on when, and from where, you scored it

The scoreboard treats every goal as one unit. That is good arithmetic and bad football. An early opener nudges the odds; a late winner from level nearly settles them; a consolation at 4–0 down changes almost nothing. This study puts a number on each of those: the expected final points of the scoring team just after the goal, minus just before it, at that scoreline and in that 15-minute window.

Unit of analysis: [loading]

Value of a state: take every minute in which a team was, say, one goal up between the 75th and the 90th, and average the points those teams finished with (3 for a win, 1 for a draw). That is what being 1–0 up late is worth. Doing it for every goal difference and every 15-minute window gives the state value table below.

Value of a goal: the state it creates minus the state it ends, in the same window. A goal from 0–0 to 1–0 late is worth the value of being one up late minus the value of being level late. The time-blind version does the same with all minutes pooled.
