RESEARCH · 02 May 2026 Top 5 Leagues + Eredivisie Expected Points Player Rating

Not All Goals Are Equal.

A goal from 0–0 to 1–0 in the fifth minute and the same goal in the eighty-fifth are not the same thing. One opens a match; the other closes it. Expected points (EP) prices every goal by what it actually moves on the league table — the difference in win/draw/loss probability before and after the ball goes in, conditioned on both the score state and the minute. Rank players by EP collected and you stop rewarding consolation hat-tricks and start rewarding the players who score in the moments that matter.

The price of a goal depends on when, and from where, you scored it

The standard scoreboard treats every goal as one unit. That is good arithmetic and bad football. Three different goals — the 1st-minute opener that swings the match probability gently toward your side, the 78th-minute equaliser that pulls a lost point out of the fire, and the 90th-minute consolation when you were already 3–0 down — should not be filed under the same column. The first is worth a fraction; the second is worth almost a full point; the third is worth, give or take, nothing.

To price them properly we model two things at once: the goal-difference transition (going from 0 to +1 is a different lever than from −1 to 0) and the minute in which it happened, binned into six 15-minute windows. For every (gd, minute_bin) pre-goal state we measure the empirical expected points the team that just experienced it ultimately collected — E[points | state]. A goal's EP value is then the difference between the post-goal state's expected points and the pre-goal state's. Late equalisers swing this number a lot. Early openers swing it a little. Goals at 4–0 do almost nothing.

Data & Method

Sources: Per-match shot tables across the top five European leagues plus the Eredivisie (FBref-derived; match_data_final_*.pkl).

Sample: [loading]

State space: Goal differences [−7, +7] × six 15-minute bins (0–15, 15–30, 30–45, 45–60, 60–75, 75–90; stoppage-time goals fold into the surrounding bins).

Value function: For each (gd, minute_bin) pre-goal state, expected final points are computed empirically as 3·pW + 1·pD. A goal's EP credit is the gap between the after-state and the before-state for the team that scored. Conceding teams get the symmetric credit on the negative side.

Attribution: Goals are credited to the goalscorer; assists to the SCA1 (first shot-creating action). A Goals+Assists view pools both. Players are pooled across leagues and seasons for the leaderboards below; the full dashboard exposes every per-league/per-season slice.

3 · The opener: worth more the longer it took to arrive

A 0→1 goal — the breaker of a deadlock — is the single most common transition in football. It is also the most striking demonstration of the time dimension. Score one before the fifteenth minute and you pick up a modest sliver of expected points; score one after the seventy-fifth and you collect over a point and a half. Eighty more minutes of football between you and the result is a lot of time for things to go wrong.

What this changes

Total goals is a starting point. EP-weighted goals is a better one. The time dimension is small in volume — across — events the absolute EP gap is bounded — but it is large in interpretation. A 78th-minute equaliser is, on the data, almost a full league point. A 6th-minute opener is a fraction of that. Anyone choosing where to spend a recruitment budget has a stronger signal once those two events are filed differently.
